Charging points in Portugal answer to two layers of law. AFIR, Regulation (EU) 2023/1804, applies
directly across the EU and sets minimum requirements for the network. DL 93/2025 and its ordinances
(portarias) are the Portuguese regime, and the old MOBI.E model runs alongside it until 31 December
2027.

:::essencial
- **AFIR**: a European regulation, which applies without any national law. It requires fast chargers along the TEN-T network, ad hoc charging at public chargers and clear prices before you charge.
- **DL 93/2025**: the legal framework for electric mobility (Regime Jurídico da Mobilidade Elétrica, RJME), amended by DL 155/2026. The 2026 ordinances cover the licence, data, insurance, power, the public domain and fees.
- **Until 31 December 2027**: the old model, with CEMEs and MOBI.E, runs alongside the new one. DL 39/2010 and the previous regime's ordinances are revoked and only matter as history.
:::

## AFIR applies without a national law {#o-que-e-o-afir}

AFIR (Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation) is Regulation (EU) 2023/1804. It sets minimum
infrastructure requirements for alternative fuels in the EU, and the electric vehicle charging network
is part of it. It replaces Directive 2014/94/EU.

The difference is in the name. A directive has to be transposed by each country into its own law. A
regulation applies directly in every Member State. AFIR is a regulation.

## What AFIR requires {#o-que-o-afir-exige}

For the charging network, there are four key points:

- **TEN-T coverage**, the trans-European transport network: fast chargers, of 150 kW or more, at
  maximum distances set along the core and the comprehensive network, with a minimum power per node.
- **Ad hoc charging**: at public chargers, you must be able to charge without a prior contract, paying
  only for that charge, and with transparent prices. [How to pay at a public charger, with or without a
  card](/carregamento-publico/).
- **Prices up front**: per kWh, per minute, per session or a combination of these, shown clearly before
  charging starts.
- **Reports**: each Member State reports its progress to the European Commission.

:::aviso
**Distances, power levels and deadlines come from the official text only.** Take them from Regulation (EU) 2023/1804 and from the Portuguese decree-laws and ordinances, never from memory or a summary. A wrong figure in a project or a tender is expensive.
:::

## In Portugal, the law is DL 93/2025 {#em-portugal-o-dl-93-2025}

DL 93/2025, of 14 August, approves the legal framework for electric mobility, the RJME. It was amended
by DL 155/2026, of 31 July. The idea is a more open, simpler and more competitive model, with a
liberalised market and universal access to charging points.

The RJME sets rules for:

- installing and operating chargers;
- renewable energy;
- smart and bidirectional charging ([what the law says about a car that gives energy
  back](/carregar-em-casa/#v2x-o-que-diz-a-lei));
- data transmission to European standards ([what the National Access Point is and what data it
  publishes](/afir-nap/)).

And it strengthens three things: operators' accountability, State oversight and protection for those
who charge.

### Two models at once, until 31 December 2027 {#ate-31-12-2027}

The old model, with CEMEs and MOBI.E, runs alongside the new one until 31 December 2027. A CEME is the
company that sells you energy on the MOBI.E network, Portugal's public charging network, through a card
or app. It was DL 155/2026 that extended the transition period to that date.

For everyday charging, see [how you pay at a charger, in the old model and the new](/carregamento-publico/).
If you operate chargers, see [what changes in grid access tariffs when a charger leaves
MOBI.E](/tarifas-de-acesso/).

## The ordinances that make the law work {#portarias-do-dl-93-2025}

The detail of DL 93/2025 sits in 2026 ordinances (portarias). Each one ties to articles of the
decree-law:

| Portaria | What it covers | DL 93/2025 |
|---|---|---|
| n.º 16/2026/1, of 12 January | The licence to operate charging points and the prior notification regime | arts. 9.º a 11.º |
| n.º 31/2026/1, of 23 January | The data OPCs make available to the EADME and its transmission to the National Access Point | art. 19.º |
| n.º 118/2026/1, of 19 March | OPCs' mandatory civil liability insurance | art. 27.º, n.ºs 2 e 3 |
| n.º 128/2026/1, of 26 March | Minimum power levels and technical rules | art. 22.º |
| n.º 130/2026/1, of 27 March | Licences for private use of the public domain | art. 8.º |
| n.º 133/2026/1, of 30 March | Fees | art. 39.º |

- **An OPC** is a charge point operator: it runs and operates chargers and sells charging to drivers.
- **The EADME** is the data aggregator for electric mobility (entidade agregadora de dados para a
  mobilidade elétrica): it receives the charger data from every OPC. [What that data is and how it
  reaches the National Access Point](/afir-nap/).
- **The minimum power levels** in Portaria n.º 128/2026/1 show up, for example, in the [minimums for new
  buildings](/carregar-em-casa/#predios-novos).

## Installations, inspections and the DGEG guide {#outros-diplomas-aplicaveis}

If you design, install or inspect, these texts also count:

- **DL 96/2017, of 10 August**, amended by Lei n.º 61/2018: the regime for private-service electrical
  installations supplied by the public service grid, the RESP (rede elétrica de serviço público). It
  ties to art. 25.º, n.º 2, of the RJME. [How it applies to a charger at
  home](/carregar-em-casa/#quem-instala-e-que-papeis-pedir).
- **Despacho DGEG n.º 22/2024, of 5 August**, an order from the DGEG, the energy authority: the
  technical and administrative procedures for the design, inspection, survey and certification of ECVE
  open to the public. An ECVE (estação de carregamento de veículos elétricos) is a charging station, one
  or more charging points in one place. The order also covers direct connection to the RESP through a
  dedicated service line, and provides standard templates for reports and information labels.
- **The Technical Guide to Electrical Installations for Supplying Electric Vehicles** (Guia Técnico das
  Instalações Elétricas para Alimentação de Veículos Elétricos), 3rd edition, from 2023: approved by the
  DGEG on 18 September 2023 and drawn up by CTE 64. [The guide's technical rules,
  explained](/instalacao-eletrica/).

## What has been revoked {#legislacao-revogada}

Before DL 93/2025, the legal framework for electric mobility was DL 39/2010. It is revoked, as are these
texts from the previous regime:

| Text | What it covered |
|---|---|
| DL 39/2010, of 26 April | The legal framework for electric mobility |
| DL 90/2014, of 11 June | The third amendment to DL 39/2010 |
| Portaria n.º 241/2015, of 12 August | The technical requirements of the operating licence |
| Portaria n.º 240/2015, of 12 August | Fees |
| Portaria n.º 231/2016, of 29 August | Mandatory civil liability insurance |

On the European side, AFIR revoked Directive 2014/94/EU. All of these help you understand the history,
not comply today. If a contract or an opinion still cites them, check what DL 93/2025 says now.

## Before you state an obligation, ask two questions {#antes-de-afirmar-uma-obrigacao}

The full, up-to-date list is on the DGEG's [Legislação Aplicável e Despachos
DGEG](https://www.dgeg.gov.pt/pt/areas-setoriais/energia/energia-eletrica/mobilidade-eletrica/legislacao-aplicavel-e-despachos-dgeg/)
page (applicable legislation and DGEG orders), in its electric mobility section, in Portuguese. With it
to hand:

1. **Is it European or Portuguese?** An AFIR obligation applies across the EU. One from DL 93/2025 or
   its ordinances applies in Portugal.
2. **Which version are you reading?** Laws change: DL 93/2025 has already been amended by DL 155/2026.
   When you cite a figure, give the source and the date of the version you checked.

## Myths and mistakes {#mitos-e-enganos}

- **"AFIR only applies once Portugal turns it into law."** It already applies. It's a regulation, not a
  directive, and applies directly in every Member State.
- **"AFIR and DL 93/2025 are the same thing."** They aren't. AFIR is European; DL 93/2025 and its
  ordinances are Portuguese law. When someone says "the law requires it", ask which one.
- **"With the new law, CEME cards are gone."** Not yet. The old model, with CEMEs and MOBI.E, runs
  alongside the new one until 31 December 2027 (DL 155/2026).
- **"No contract, no charging at a public charger."** AFIR requires public chargers to allow ad hoc
  charging: no prior contract, paying only for that charge.
- **"You see the price later, on the bill."** AFIR requires the price, per kWh, per minute or per
  session, to be clear before charging starts.
- **"The DL 39/2010 rules still apply."** They're revoked, as are the 2015 and 2016 ordinances in the
  table above. Today, DL 93/2025 is the law.
