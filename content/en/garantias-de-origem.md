A guarantee of origin (garantia de origem, GO) proves that 1 MWh was produced from renewable sources,
and it's how an electricity supplier (comercializador) shows, on the bill, that the energy is green. It
proves a purchase, not a flow: it doesn't say that this electricity is what reached your car, or that
it was produced at the hour you charged, or that the power plant is new. Even so, it's the only legal
instrument for proving origin on the bill.

:::essencial
- **To check a "100 % renewable" claim**, look at the offer's label and the supplier's mix on the bill and on the website of ERSE, the energy regulator. At a public charger, ask the OPC (charge point operator) where the GOs come from and what period they cover.
- **Covering your car with GOs is cheap**: at Portuguese auction prices, the GOs for a year of charging cost very little. A green tariff can cost more, because the supplier adds its margin.
- **The CO2 credits for electric cars don't exist yet**: they're in DL 93/2025, but the ordinance (portaria) that creates them hasn't been published, and until then nobody issues them and there's no price.
:::

## A GO proves a purchase, not the energy's path {#o-que-uma-go-prova}

A guarantee of origin is an electronic document that proves to the final customer that 1 MWh was
produced from renewable sources (DL 84/2022, art. 29.º, n.º 1). It lets the supplier prove the
renewable share of its portfolio (cabaz), the mix of sources behind the energy it sells.

When the supplier or the charger's operator sells you renewable energy backed by GOs, this is what it
means: they cancelled renewable GOs equal to your consumption, for the period those GOs cover. It
doesn't mean the car "charged on solar power".

### What a GO doesn't prove {#o-que-uma-go-nao-prova}

- **That the plant is new.** A GO from a decades-old dam is worth the same as one from a new solar
  park. Buying GOs doesn't, by itself, fund new renewable production. If that's what you want (what's
  called additionality), the way to get it is a contract with a new plant, a PPA, or
  self-consumption.
- **That the hour matches.** A GO covers a production period, a month for example, not the hour the
  car charged. Charging at 3 in the morning with a solar GO is legal, but it doesn't mean the sun was
  producing.

None of this makes GOs worthless. They're the only legal instrument for proving origin on the bill,
and they stop the same renewable energy from being sold twice as green.

### One GO per MWh, and it can only be used once {#uma-go-por-mwh}

- **Each MWh can only have one GO** (art. 29.º, n.º 2).
- **A GO can be sold separately from the energy** it came from (art. 28.º, n.º 3). In that case, that
  energy can no longer be counted as renewable in the seller's portfolio (n.º 4). That's what prevents
  double counting.
- **It's valid for 12 months** after the MWh is produced, and must be cancelled no later than 6 months
  after its validity ends (art. 29.º, n.º 5). Once used to prove origin, it's cancelled (n.º 6).
- **It doesn't count towards the national renewable targets** (art. 28.º, n.º 2).
- **It works across borders.** GOs issued in other Member States are recognised in Portugal, unless
  there are well-founded doubts (art. 29.º, n.º 7). REN, which issues GOs on the mainland, is
  connected to the AIB, the European association of issuing bodies, which allows GOs to be imported
  and exported.

Each GO states its type and source, the start and end dates of production and the installation:
identification, location, type and capacity (art. 29.º, n.º 4). It also states whether it received
investment aid or other support, its date of entry into service, the country and the issuing body,
and it carries a unique number.

There are also GOs for high-efficiency cogeneration, for renewable gases and for low-carbon gases
(arts. 30.º a 32.º).

## "100 % renewable": how to check {#como-confirmar}

### At a public charger {#num-posto-publico}

The OPC, whoever runs the charger, buys its electricity from a supplier (DL 93/2025, art. 9.º). If it
advertises 100 % renewable energy, that can only rest on GOs cancelled by it or by its supplier, or on
self-consumption on site (art. 15.º, f)).

Ask the OPC where the GOs come from and what period they cover.

### At home {#em-casa}

At home, the car charges on the energy from your home contract, or from the
[sub-CPE, a delivery point just for the car](/sub-cpe/), if you have one. The label that counts is the
one from that contract's supplier.

### On the bill, the label says where the energy comes from {#na-fatura-a-rotulagem}

The supplier must state, on the bill or in the documents that come with it, how much each source
contributed to the energy supplied and the associated CO2 emissions (DL 15/2022, art. 136.º, n.º 3, e
Anexo VII, n.º 3). This is called labelling (rotulagem), and the rules are in ERSE's Diretiva n.º
16/2018. The label shows:

- the mix for the quarter, for the last four quarters and for the previous year;
- the specific emissions for the previous year and for each bill.

ERSE publishes the "system base mix" (mix base do sistema): it's what a supplier shows when it proves
nothing with GOs. It also has a labelling simulator.

**A "100 % renewable" offer is only that on the label if the supplier has cancelled GOs, or used
physical bilateral contracts, for all of that consumption.** To check, look at the offer's label and
the supplier's mix on ERSE's website and on the bill.

## What it costs to make your car green {#quanto-custa}

Very little, at auction prices. The maths: the price of a GO per MWh, divided by 10, gives the cost in
cents per kWh. Multiply by the kWh your car charges in a year. Use the price from the
[latest auction](/garantias-de-origem/#os-numeros-dos-leiloes), with its date: GO prices varied more
than fortyfold between 2022 and 2025.

That's the wholesale price in Portugal. A "green" tariff can cost more than that, because the supplier
adds its margin.

## Solar panels at home: the car's energy is already yours {#autoconsumo}

With self-consumption (autoconsumo), the energy you use at home, including what goes into the car,
doesn't need GOs: it's already yours. A self-consumer can request GOs for the surplus electricity they
feed into the grid (DL 15/2022, art. 88.º, n.º 1, h)).

:::aviso
**Do the maths before requesting GOs for your panels.** Compare the registration cost and the fees of the EEGO, the body that issues GOs, which are set by ERSE, with what the GOs for your surplus MWh would earn at the latest auction price. For a home, with few MWh a year, it rarely pays off.
:::

How to use surplus solar to charge the car during the day is in
[charging at home with solar panels](/carregar-em-casa/#com-paineis-solares).

## Fleets: GOs go into the emissions report {#frotas}

A company that reports the emissions from its electricity use with the market-based method uses the
GOs cancelled in its name to lower the emission factor of that consumption. In a sustainability
report, this includes the electricity for fleet cars that is billed to the company.

Fleet taxation and employees charging at home are covered in [companies and fleets](/empresas-e-frotas/).

## CO2 credits: in the law, but they don't exist yet {#titulos-de-co2}

DL 93/2025, art. 5.º, provides for the CO2 emissions avoided with renewable electricity in electric
mobility to be given a value. People who use electric mobility and operators would benefit from
tradable credits (títulos), which count towards the targets for renewables in fuels and transport.

:::aviso
**There are no CO2 credits to sell yet.** The ordinance that creates them hasn't been published: in November 2026 it wasn't on the list of the DGEG, the energy authority. Until it is, nobody issues them, there's no price and no way to apply for them. Be wary of anyone who tells you otherwise.
:::

They aren't GOs. A GO proves where electricity comes from; these credits would count towards the
transport targets.

## GO-PT auctions: the DGEG sells the GOs you've already paid for in your tariffs {#os-leiloes-go-pt}

### Where the GOs come from {#de-onde-vem-as-go}

A producer with a guaranteed tariff, other direct price support, an investment incentive, an energy
purchase contract (contrato de aquisição de energia, CAE) or an agreement to terminate a CAE **must hand its GOs over to the DGEG**.
Without that, it doesn't get paid (DL 84/2022, art. 28.º, n.º 9).

Producers under a subsidised remuneration regime can't sell their GOs separately (n.º 5). The
exception is the winners of grid injection capacity auctions, when their regime is a gain for the
National Electricity System (SEN), which is checked periodically (n.os 6 a 8).

In other words: the GOs from wind, hydro, solar and renewable thermal power that consumers already pay
for through their tariffs belong to the SEN. The DGEG is the one that sells them.

### How they work {#como-funcionam-os-leiloes}

- **An electronic auction**, on the OMIP platform, held monthly, quarterly or every six months
  (Despacho n.º 6560-B/2021, n.º 1). In 2026 there are six a year, and in 2027 there are five.
- **The call comes out at least 20 days ahead.** The DGEG publishes the date, the quantities per
  technology and production period (the "lots"), the reserve price, the participation costs and any
  restrictions (n.os 4 e 5).
- **Only legal entities can take part**: registered with the EEGO, admitted as participants at OMIP
  and qualified with a financial guarantee up to two working days before the auction, under OMIP's
  rules. A private individual doesn't buy GOs at the auction.
- **Results come out per lot**, at a marginal price. The EEGO transfers the GOs to the winners'
  accounts (Despacho, n.º 14), and OMIP publishes the history of the results.
- **Running the auction has a cost.** OMIP and the supplier of last resort (CUR) get a fee per MWh
  awarded, capped at a percentage of the revenue (Despacho, n.os 11 e 12).

### Where the money goes {#para-onde-vai-a-receita}

The auctions' net result is **deducted from the extra costs of buying renewable electricity with
guaranteed remuneration**, under ERSE's Tariff Regulation (DL 84/2022, art. 28.º, n.º 10; Despacho,
n.º 13). Those extra costs go into the [grid access tariffs](/tarifas-de-acesso/#o-que-e-a-tar) that
everyone pays. So the auction revenue lowers everyone's bill, a little.

How much it lowers a household's bill isn't known: no official per-customer figures are published.

The revenue depends on the European GO price, not on the quantity, which stays steady from auction to
auction. It peaked in 2023 and fell sharply in 2024 and 2025, when GO prices fell in Europe.

## Who issues, who regulates, who sells {#quem-faz-o-que}

| Who | Role | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| EEGO, the issuing body: REN on the mainland, EDA in the Azores, EEM in Madeira | Registers producers, issues, transfers and cancels GOs, audits installations | DL 15/2022, arts. 176.º e 294.º |
| ERSE | Regulates the EEGO, approves its procedures manual and budget, sets the EEGO's fees, defines labelling | DL 15/2022, arts. 174.º, n.º 4, 178.º e 179.º; DL 84/2022, art. 28.º, n.º 11 |
| DGEG | Receives the GOs from supported production and sells them at auction | DL 84/2022, art. 28.º, n.os 9 e 10 |
| OMIP | Runs the GO-PT auctions and manages participants' financial guarantees | Despacho n.º 6560-B/2021 |
| SU Eletricidade (CUR) | Invoices and settles the auction revenue | Despacho n.º 6560-B/2021, n.º 13 |
| ENSE | Supervises the EEGO | DL 84/2022, art. 33.º, n.º 5 |

- **REN is the EEGO on a transitional basis.** The law provides for a tender for the EEGO licence,
  for a maximum of 10 years (DL 15/2022, arts. 175.º e 294.º). There hasn't been a tender yet.
- **The EEGO's procedures manual** must comply with the EN 16325 standard (DL 15/2022, art. 174.º,
  n.º 4, as amended by DL 130/2026).
- **To register and request GOs**, a producer uses the EEGO portal, at REN.

## Myths and mistakes {#mitos-e-enganos}

- **"My car charged on renewable energy."** A GO doesn't prove that. It proves that the OPC or its
  supplier cancelled renewable GOs equal to your consumption, for the period they cover.
- **"The GO is solar, so I charged on sunshine."** A GO covers a production period, not the hour you
  charged. At 3 in the morning, a solar GO is legal, but it doesn't say the sun was producing.
- **"If I pay more for green, I'm funding new plants."** Not by itself. A GO from a decades-old dam is
  worth the same as one from a new solar park. For new production, it's a PPA with a new plant or
  self-consumption.
- **"GOs are just paper."** They're the only legal instrument for proving origin on the bill, and they
  stop the same renewable energy from being sold twice as green (DL 84/2022, art. 28.º, n.os 3 e 4).
- **"GOs count towards the country's renewable targets."** They don't (DL 84/2022, art. 28.º, n.º 2).
  They let the supplier prove the renewable share of its portfolio.
- **"With panels at home, I need GOs for the car."** You don't: the energy you use at home is already
  yours. The GOs you can request are for the surplus you feed into the grid, and for a home they
  rarely pay off.
- **"I can already sell my car's CO2 credits."** Not yet. They're in DL 93/2025, art. 5.º, but the
  ordinance that creates them hasn't been published. And they aren't GOs.
- **"The rules are on the ENSE page."** ENSE's 2019 page on GOs is out of date: it cites DL 141/2010
  and DL 23/2010. Since 2022, the basis is DL 84/2022 (arts. 28.º a 33.º) and DL 15/2022 (arts. 174.º
  a 179.º e 294.º).
