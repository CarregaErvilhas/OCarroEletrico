In a company entitled to deduct VAT (IVA), a 100 % electric car within the cost limit set by the
portaria (a ministerial order) gets the VAT back on the purchase and on the electricity, and pays no
autonomous taxation (tributação autónoma), IUC or ISV. The VAT on the wallbox, though, can't be
deducted, according to the AT, the tax authority. And when the car charges at the driver's home, what decides the VAT is the name on the bill.

:::essencial
- **One limit decides three taxes**: VAT on the purchase, depreciation and autonomous taxation on a 100 % electric car all depend on the same cost limit, excluding VAT.
- **The wallbox stays out**: according to the AT, VAT on the charger can't be deducted, and neither can VAT on maintenance, tolls or insurance. VAT on electricity can, with the invoice in the company's name.
- **At the driver's home, the invoice decides**: reimbursing the employee is simple, but deducts no VAT. A sub-CPE in the company's name puts the invoice in the right name, with fixed costs, on a reading of the rules still to be confirmed.
:::

## Six questions that change the answer {#seis-perguntas}

"Is it worth it?" has different answers depending on the company. Answer these before running the
numbers, and take them to your accountant.

1. **What kind of entity?** A company, a self-employed person with organised accounts (contabilidade organizada), an IPSS (a private social-welfare institution) or a local authority (autarquia).
2. **Is it entitled to deduct VAT?** An exempt activity, such as much of healthcare, education and insurance, doesn't deduct VAT. In that case, the VAT rules on this page don't apply.
3. **Purchase, finance lease (locação financeira) or renting (long-term rental)?** It changes who deducts what, and from which invoice.
4. **100 % electric or plug-in hybrid?** The limits and the rules aren't the same.
5. **Where will it charge?** At the company's premises, on the public network or at the driver's home.
6. **Is there personal use?** If the driver takes the car home and uses it outside work, the sums change.

## What you can deduct: one limit rules three taxes {#o-que-se-deduz}

What follows are the rules and where they come from, not tax advice. Before deciding, check your case
with your certified accountant (contabilista certificado). Several rules come from binding rulings
(informações vinculativas) by the AT, the Autoridade Tributária. Each one answers a specific case: it
stands as the AT's position, not as law.

### The cost limit {#o-limite-de-custo}

For 100 % electric passenger cars, Portaria n.º 467/2010, amended by Lei n.º 82-D/2014, sets a limit on
the acquisition cost, excluding VAT. The amount is in the facts, further down. The same limit counts
for three things:

- **VAT on the purchase or the lease** is deductible if the acquisition cost stays within the limit.
- **Depreciation for corporate income tax (IRC)** is accepted up to the limit. Anything above it
  doesn't count, says the Código do IRC, art. 34.º, n.º 1, e).
- **Autonomous taxation** isn't charged on the expenses of a 100 % electric car within the limit.
  Above it, it's charged at the rate shown in the facts.

**Going over the limit costs you all the VAT.** Above it, the AT considers that no part of the VAT on
the purchase is deductible, not just the part over the limit. Work out the cost excluding VAT before
you close the deal.

**Plug-in hybrids** have their own limit and their own autonomous-taxation rules. If you don't find
them in the facts below, check them in the portaria and in the Código do IRC.

### IUC, ISV and electricity {#iuc-isv-e-eletricidade}

- **IUC** (the annual circulation tax): 100 % electric cars are exempt.
- **ISV** (the vehicle tax): they're outside it, which matters when importing a used electric car.
- **Electricity**: VAT on the electricity used by electric cars and plug-in hybrids is deductible, with
  the invoice in the company's name. It's in the Código do IVA, art. 21.º, n.º 2, h).

## What you can't deduct: the wallbox, the garage and personal use {#o-que-nao-se-deduz}

:::aviso
**VAT on the wallbox can't be deducted.** According to the AT, VAT on buying and installing a wallbox for the company's cars isn't deductible, because it's an expense of using the cars. Many people assume the opposite: take it out of your sums before deciding.
:::

- **Running costs** stay out too: repairs, maintenance, tolls and insurance. The VAT exception covers
  only the purchase and the electricity.
- **Personal use has a cost.** If the car is used outside the business, VAT is due on that use. For
  personal income tax (IRS), personal use of a company car may count as income in kind (rendimento em
  espécie) for the driver. We don't know of a rule specific to electric cars: check with your
  accountant.

## Charging the fleet: three places, three sets of rules {#carregar-a-frota}

### At the premises, the company is the DPC {#nas-instalacoes}

A charger that isn't open to the public makes the company the DPC (detentor de ponto de
carregamento), the holder of a private charger (DL 93/2025, art. 21.º). A car park only for staff or
customers stays private.

- **Opening it to the public changes everything.** If the car park opens to the general public, the
  charger becomes public and the company needs an OPC (charge point operator) licence. See [the rules
  for public chargers](/afir/) and [what the law asks of someone with a private
  charger](/carregar-em-casa/#quem-instala-e-que-papeis-pedir).
- **The electricity comes on the company's bill**, and its VAT is deductible. The charger's isn't.
- **The installation follows the technical guide of the DGEG**, the energy authority, for shared
  charging areas. The circuit, the protections and the boards are in [electrical
  installation](/instalacao-eletrica/).

### On the public network, everything in the company's name {#na-rede-publica}

- **A card or app in the company's name**, with invoices in the company's name. One from an OPC works,
  or one from an eMSP, the mobility service provider that sells charging at several OPCs' chargers.
  Until 31 December 2027, a CEME card also works: the CEME is the supplier that sells the energy on
  the MOBI.E network.
- **Ask your accountant** whether a charging invoice counts as "electricity used" for VAT. The AT talks
  about electricity, and an OPC's or an eMSP's invoice may bundle services.
- **If the company reports its fleet's emissions**, "100 % renewable" electricity only counts if
  guarantees of origin have been cancelled for that consumption. [How "100 % renewable" is
  proven](/garantias-de-origem/).

### At the driver's home, the invoice decides {#em-casa-de-quem-conduz}

The problem is the invoice. According to the AT, VAT on the electricity charged at the employee's home
is only deductible if the invoice is in the company's name. There are three ways to do it:

1. **Reimbursing the employee.** The consumption stays on the home contract, and the company pays the employee what the car used, measured by the wallbox's meter. This is the agreed model (modelo acordado), one of the [ways to split off the car's bill](/sub-cpe/), and the simplest. But the company doesn't deduct the VAT, because the invoice is in the employee's name. How the reimbursement is treated for IRC and IRS is one for your accountant.
2. **The company as DPC, with a sub-CPE in its name.** The company buys and installs the wallbox at the employee's home and becomes the DPC of that charger. As DPC, it can ask the grid operator for a sub-CPE, an internal delivery point with its own code. It needs written authorisation from the holder of the home's contract (GMLDD, art. 11.º-A, n.os 1 e 2, ERSE's metering guide). Then it contracts the electricity for that point in the company's name. The sub-CPE invoice is in the company's name, which is the AT's condition for deducting VAT. The rest of the home stays on the employee's contract.
3. **A charger integrated into MOBI.E**, with the company's CEME card, while the transitional period lasts, until 31 December 2027. The CEME's invoice comes in the company's name. It ends with the end of the old regime.

| Option | What it gives the company | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| Reimbursing the employee | The simplest solution | Giving up the VAT: the invoice is the employee's |
| Sub-CPE in the company's name | The invoice in the company's name, the AT's condition for deducting VAT | The holder's written authorisation, a meter, adapting the installation, certification and a new fixed charge |
| Charger integrated into MOBI.E | The CEME's invoice in the company's name | A company CEME card, and only until 31 December 2027 |

:::aviso
**A sub-CPE in the company's name is a reading, not a recipe.** It's our reading of the rules of ERSE, the energy regulator, and of the AT: neither of them has described this solution. Before going ahead, check it with the grid operator and with your accountant.
:::

### A sub-CPE at the employee's home: what it costs and what it limits {#sub-cpe-o-que-custa}

- **It has fixed costs.** There's the regulated price of the meter, adapting the installation,
  certification and a new fixed charge on the sub-CPE's bill. It pays off more with many kilometres a
  month; for light use, reimbursement is usually enough. Run the numbers with the prices of your case,
  not from memory.
- **It gives no more power** than the home has, and a cut to the home's contract cuts the charger.
- **It isn't immediate.** The grid operator has up to two months to install the meter. [How to apply
  for a sub-CPE, step by step](/sub-cpe/).
- **In a building, the condominium (condomínio) is still notified.** Installing at a garage space needs
  the [prior notice to the condominium](/carregar-em-casa/#comunicacao-previa) (DL 93/2025, art.
  23.º).
- **Who authorises the sub-CPE depends on the board** the charger is fed from: the employee, if it
  comes from their unit; the condominium, if it comes from the common services. See [which board feeds
  each space](/carregar-em-casa/#de-que-quadro-sai-a-eletricidade) and the diagrams in [electrical
  installation](/instalacao-eletrica/).
- **Put in writing** who owns the charger and what happens when the employee leaves the company or
  moves house: removing the charger, selling it to the employee, and ending the sub-CPE contract.
- **With MOBI.E chargers at the same installation**, creating a sub-CPE means de-integrating every
  charger at that installation from the platform of the EGME, MOBI.E's managing entity.

## Myths and mistakes {#mitos-e-enganos}

- **"VAT on the wallbox is deductible, like the car's."** No, according to the AT: it's an expense of
  using the cars. What's deductible is the VAT on buying the car and on the electricity.
- **"If the car goes over the limit, you only lose the VAT on the excess."** You lose all of it. Above
  the portaria's limit, the AT considers that no part of the VAT on the purchase is deductible.
- **"Paying the employee for home electricity lets you deduct the VAT."** It doesn't. The invoice is in
  their name, and the AT requires an invoice in the company's name.
- **"A car park only for staff has public chargers."** It stays private, and the company is the DPC (DL 93/2025, art. 21.º).
  It only becomes public if it opens to the general public.
- **"With a sub-CPE, the car charges with more power."** It doesn't. A sub-CPE gives no more power than
  the home has.
- **"A '100 % renewable' contract is enough for the emissions report."** It only counts with guarantees
  of origin cancelled for that consumption.
